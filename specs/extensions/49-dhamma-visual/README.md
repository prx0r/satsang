# DHAMMA-VISUAL — the visual language (2026-10-20, extension pack 49)

A complete visual grammar for Buddhism. Not individual figures — a
language. Once you understand the grammar, you can read any visualization.
Designed for the 33 sermons megaseries and everything after.

## The principle

Every Buddhist concept has a physical form. Not a symbol. Not a diagram.
A form — something with mass, joints, materials, and behaviour. The form
IS the concept. When you see the form move, you understand the teaching.

## The visual alphabet

Five materials. Every visualization is built from these.

| material | colour | meaning | behaviour |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Light** | warm gold (#e8cf96) | awareness, consciousness, what remains | always present, never manufactured, dims when obscured |
| **Metal** | dark steel (#2a3040) | fabrication, construction, saṅkhāra | rigid, joins to other metal, wears over time |
| **Glass** | transparent, coloured | signs, nimitta, perception filters | refracts light, colours what passes through, can be removed |
| **Water** | deep blue (#1a2a3a) | feeling, vedanā, sensation | always responds, never still unless input stops |
| **Stone** | grey (#4a5060) | ground, reality, the unconditioned | never moves, never changes, always there |

## The mechanical vocabulary

Six mechanisms. Every process is one of these.

| mechanism | physical form | dhamma meaning | example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gear** | toothed wheel that drives another | dependent arising — A triggers B | the twelve links |
| **Lens** | coloured glass that filters light | sign, nimitta — perception is filtered | how we see |
| **Spring** | coiled metal that pushes or pulls | craving, taṇhā — the wanting | desire, aversion |
| **Valve** | gate that opens and closes | clinging, upādāna — holding on | grasping, release |
| **Pivot** | point that turns a state | becoming, bhava — the shift | birth, death |
| **Cup** | vessel that catches what falls | suffering, dukkha — what accumulates | the result |

## The spatial grammar

Where things sit tells you what they are.

| position | meaning |
|---|---|
| **Deep (bottom)** | fundamental, root, the first cause |
| **Shallow (top)** | surface, result, what you notice first |
| **Left** | earlier in the chain, past, cause |
| **Right** | later in the chain, future, effect |
| **Centre** | the subject, what you're looking at |
| **Edge** | context, background, what's around |
| **Circular** | recursion, self-reference, the observer |
| **Spiral** | proliferation, papañca, complexity growing |

## The temporal grammar

How things move tells you what's happening.

| motion | meaning |
|---|---|
| **Rotation** | ongoing process, the wheel turning |
| **Oscillation** | craving/aversion cycle, the pendulum |
| **Stillness** | cessation, nibbāna, the machine stopped |
| **Fade** | impermanence, anicca, things passing |
| **Cascade** | dependent arising, one thing triggering the next |
| **Convergence** | things coming together, conditions meeting |

## The light rule

Light (awareness) is always present. It's never manufactured. It dims
when glass (signs) are in front of it. When you remove the glass, the
light is brighter — not because you added light, but because you removed
the filter. That's the signless: the light was always there.

## The stillness rule

When a machine stops, the base plate is still there. The mounts are
still there. The holes where the gears were are still there. Nothing
moves. That's nibbāna: not absence, stillness. The machine is off. The
ground remains.

## Two modes: third-person and first-person

The language has two modes. The 33 sermons are Ñāṇananda's actual
voice, live-recorded. His words are the guide. The visuals are what
you see while he speaks. We don't write a script — we visualise around
his words.

### Third-person (isometric) — for mechanisms

You're looking at a machine on a bench. You click a gear. You understand
the mechanism. This is for dependent arising, for structure, for how
things connect. The twelve-link machine lives here. The anatomy skill
builds these.

### First-person (immersive) — for experience

You're inside the teaching. The screen fills with what's happening.
The voice describes it and you're already in it. This is for papañca,
for the signs, for the experience of the noisy mind. You don't look at
the vortex — you're in it.

**The vortex, first person:**
1. Screen is still. Clean. Empty.
2. Something lands — a sound, a sensation, one ripple.
3. The ripple spirals outward. Fills the screen. You're inside it.
4. Ñāṇananda's voice: "and this is how one contact becomes a life."
5. The spiral keeps going. Complexity. Noise. The mind doing what it does.
6. The spiral starts to thin. Slowly. Not because you made it slow.
   Because the input stopped.
7. Underneath: stillness. What was always there. The practice.

**The sign-lens, first person:**
1. You see a room. Through coloured glass. Everything tinted.
2. The voice: "every perception comes with a sign."
3. A lens is removed. The colour shifts. The room looks different.
4. Another lens removed. Another shift.
5. All lenses gone. The room is just the room. The light is just light.
6. The voice: "that's what was always there."

## The 33 sermons as a visual score

Each of Ñāṇananda's 33 lectures gets a visualization. The sermons
progress through the path, so the visualizations progress through the
grammar:

**Sermons 1-5: The introduction** — What nibbāna is and isn't
- Visual: the empty machine (Fig 5) — base plate, no gears
- Teaches: nibbāna is not a state. It's what remains.

**Sermons 6-10: The three characteristics** — Anicca, dukkha, anattā
- Visual: the fading gear (metal fading to stone) — impermanence
- Visual: the cup filling (water accumulating in the cup) — suffering
- Visual: the missing observer (camera watching a mirror that cracks) — no-self

**Sermons 11-16: Dependent arising** — The twelve links
- Visual: the twelve-link machine (Fig 1) — gears in a chain
- Each sermon highlights one link, shows what triggers it, what it triggers

**Sermons 17-22: Papañca and signs** — How the mind proliferates
- Visual: the vortex (Fig 3) — one drop spiralling into complexity
- Visual: the sign-lens (Fig 2) — coloured filters over light

**Sermons 23-28: Signless concentration** — Animitta samādhi
- Visual: removing the lenses one by one — light getting brighter
- Visual: the gap between signs — empty space between gears

**Sermons 29-33: What remains** — Nibbāna as here-and-now
- Visual: the still machine (Fig 5) — nothing moves, ground remains
- Visual: the light without filters — what was always there

## The series title

Not "how to attain nibbāna." Not "the 33 sermons explained."

**"The Machine That Stops"** — or whatever the trojan horse title is.
The viewer comes for something else. They see the machine. They click
a gear. It stops. They realise: that's nibbāna.

## Why this is a magnum opus

Because it's not a video series. It's a visual language for the entire
path. Once you've seen the twelve-link machine, you can read any other
visualization in the language. The gears mean the same thing everywhere.
The lenses mean the same thing. The stillness means the same thing.

You're not teaching Buddhism with pictures. You're building a language
that Buddhism speaks through.
