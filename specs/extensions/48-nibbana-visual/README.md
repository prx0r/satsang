# NIBBANA-VISUAL — the megaseries (2026-10-20, extension pack 48)

Using wheresryan22/anatomy (8100 stars, MIT) to model dependent arising,
nibbāna, perception, signs, and the vortex as interactive isometric
figures. The skill's core idea: "the object is the explanation." Don't
draw boxes and arrows. Invent a physical thing whose working IS the
concept.

## The trojan horse

The first episode is not "how to attain nibbāna." It's something people
click for other reasons — what to focus on, what to do with your life,
why meditation feels pointless. Then the anatomy figure reveals the
machine underneath. The viewer realises: this is dependent arising. This
is what the Buddha actually taught. And nibbāna is what happens when you
remove one gear.

## The figures

### Fig 1: The Twelve-Link Machine (dependent arising)

**The object:** A Rube Goldberg machine on a workbench. A ball drops in
at "ignorance" and rolls through twelve mechanisms — each one triggers
the next. At the end, the ball falls into a cup labelled "suffering."

**The interaction:** Hover over any link to see it highlighted. Click a
link to remove it — the machine stops. The ball freezes. Nothing beyond
that link moves. That's nibbāna: not a state, a stopped machine.

**What it teaches:** Dependent arising is not a philosophy. It's a
mechanism. Each link is physical — it triggers the next. Remove one and
the whole chain halts.

**Parts:** ~80 — base plate, twelve mechanism assemblies (gear, lever,
spring, cam, ratchet, pawl, flywheel, belt, pulley, chain, sprocket,
escapement), ball track, catch cup, label plates, corner screws.

### Fig 2: The Sign-Lens (nimitta / signless)

**The object:** A camera obscura or a lens bench. Light enters from one
side. Before it reaches the "eye" (a glass disc), it passes through a
series of coloured lenses — each lens is a sign (concept, label,
category). The image on the screen is coloured by the lenses, not by
the light itself.

**The interaction:** Remove a lens — the colour on the screen shifts.
Remove all lenses — white light hits the screen. That's the signless:
not nothing, the thing that was always there, unfiltered.

**What it teaches:** Every perception comes with a sign. The sign is
made, not given. When signs stop, what remains is nibbāna — the light
that was always there.

**Parts:** ~60 — lens bench, rail, lens holders, coloured lenses, screen,
light source, adjustment knobs, ruler strip.

### Fig 3: The Papañca Vortex (proliferation)

**The object:** A whirlpool in a glass tank. A single drop falls in
(sense contact). It hits the water and spirals outward — each turn
generates more motion, more complexity. The vortex grows until it fills
the tank.

**The interaction:** Tap to drop a second hit — two vortices merge and
turbulence increases. The only way to calm the water: stop adding drops.
The vortex doesn't stop because you force it. It stops because the
input stops.

**What it teaches:** Papañca is the mind's habit of turning one experience
into a story into an emotion into a life. You don't stop the story. You
see it being made. The making slows when you stop feeding it.

**Parts:** ~50 — glass tank, water plane, drop mechanism, spiral guides,
measurement grid, drain valve.

### Fig 4: The Observer Machine (the witness)

**The object:** A machine that watches itself. A camera on a rail points
at a mirror. Inside the mirror, another camera points back. The
recursion goes three levels deep before it hits a wall.

**The interaction:** Hover to follow the recursion. At the third level,
the mirror cracks. Behind it: nothing. No camera. No observer. Just the
room.

**What it teaches:** The observer is itself a fabrication. When you look
for the one who watches, you find seeing but not a seer. The recursion
breaks. What remains is the room — bare awareness without a witness.

**Parts:** ~70 — camera bodies, rail, mirror frames, mirror surfaces,
recursion brackets, room walls, light.

### Fig 5: What Remains (nibbāna)

**The object:** The same machine from Fig 1, but empty. All twelve gears
removed. The base plate is still there. The mounts are still there. The
holes where the gears were are still there. But nothing moves. Nothing
triggers. Nothing suffers.

**The interaction:** Nothing. That's the point. The figure is still.
Hover does nothing. Click does nothing. The machine is off.

**What it teaches:** Nibbāna is not a state you achieve. It is what
remains when the fabrications are gone. The base plate was always there.
You just couldn't see it under the machine.

## The series structure (33 sermons → episodes)

Each of Ñāṇananda's 33 lectures becomes an episode. Each episode has:
1. The trojan horse opening (relatable hook)
2. The anatomy figure (the machine that IS the concept)
3. The TTS voice (owner's doctrine — play, not meditation)
4. The practice (timer-native, from packs 43-45)
5. The stage (from the NANDI game — which fabrication this episode sees through)

| ep | sermon topic | figure | stage |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Why nibbāna is real and urgent | Fig 1 (twelve-link machine) | intro |
| 2 | What nibbāna is NOT | Fig 5 (empty machine) | waiting-room |
| 3 | The mind that makes signs | Fig 2 (sign-lens) | the-sign |
| 4 | Papañca: the story machine | Fig 3 (vortex) | story-machine |
| 5 | The three characteristics as lived | Fig 1 (highlight anicca) | identity-view |
| 6 | Dependent arising: the mechanism | Fig 1 (full chain) | concept-web |
| 7 | Signless concentration | Fig 2 (remove all lenses) | the-signless |
| 8 | The observer falls | Fig 4 (mirror recursion) | observer-falls |
| 9 | What remains | Fig 5 (empty machine) | what-remains |
| ... | (remaining 24 sermons map to the 12 stages + practice episodes) | | |

## Production pipeline

1. Build each figure with the anatomy skill (Node build scripts)
2. Capture with `scripts/capture.mjs` for video stills
3. Drive with `scripts/drive.mjs` for interaction sequences
4. TTS the doctrine voice (Fish Audio or any TTS)
5. Edit: figure animation + TTS + practice prompt
6. Publish: YouTube (figure animation) + satsang (interactive figure)

## Why this works as a trojan horse

The first video is not "how to attain nibbāna." It's "why meditation
feels pointless" or "what to focus on in a post-work world" or "the
science of why you can't stop thinking." The anatomy figure shows the
machine. The viewer clicks a gear. The machine stops. The viewer
realises: that's what the Buddha meant. That's what nibbāna is. And
there's a practice that takes you there.

The title is irrelevant. The figure does the teaching.
