# NANDI — the game (2026-10-20, extension pack 47)

**Named after Nandī, the gatekeeper who lets through only those ready.**

Take all of Ñāṇananda's work and model it as stages. Make it a game.
Not meditation — play. The game is: see through the fabrication, and
what was hidden becomes visible. The boss is the fabrication itself.
The reward is what remains when you stop fabricating.

## The three works as three game worlds

Ñāṇananda wrote three books that map to three worlds:

### World 1: Concept and Reality — "The Proliferation"
The mind's habit of expanding every experience into a story (papañca).
You don't stop the story. You see it being made.

**Stages:**
1. **The Story Machine** — notice when experience becomes narrative.
   "And then" — the mind always reaching for the next thing.
   Practice: when you notice a story starting, say "story" and return
   to bare experience. The story continues. You just see it.
2. **The Label** — the mind naming everything. "Sound." "Thought."
   "Me." The label is a sign (nimitta) that obscures bare experience.
   Practice: notice the label being applied. See the label itself.
   Rest in the experience behind the label.
3. **The Concept Web** — how one concept leads to another. "Sound" →
   "fan" → "broken" → "need to fix" → "landlord" → "anger." One label
   becomes a story becomes an emotion becomes a life.
   Practice: notice the web expanding. Don't cut it. Watch it spin.

### World 2: Magic of the Mind — "The Signless"
The signless concentration (animitta samādhi). When signs stop, what
remains is nibbāna. This is the core practice world.

**Stages:**
4. **The Sign** — every perception comes with a sign: a concept, a
   label, a category. The sign is made, not given. See the making.
5. **The Gap Between Signs** — between one sign and the next, there
   is a gap. Rest there. This is the doorway.
6. **The Signless** — when signs stop, what remains? Not nothing.
   Something that was always there, obscured by the signs.
   Practice: sound-dissolution. Trace the sound to the silence.
   The silence was always there.

### World 3: Nibbāna: The Mind Stilled — "The Stillness"
The 33 lectures. The progressive stilling of the mind. Nibbāna as
here-and-now experience, not a metaphysical elsewhere.

**Stages:**
7. **The Noisy Mind** — you don't even know what quiet sounds like.
   The mind is so noisy, silence is unthinkable.
8. **The Waiting Room** — "Am I doing it right? Why am I not seeing it?"
   The waiting-room mind. Name it. That's the practice.
9. **The Impossible** — you can't force the mind to submit. You can't
   not try either. The impossibility IS the teaching.
10. **The Softening** — something softens. Not because you made it
    soften. Because you stopped making it hard.
11. **The Observer Falls** — the one who was watching is itself a
    sign. When the observer is seen through, what remains?
12. **What Remains** — nibbāna. Not a state. What is left when you
    stop fabricating. The game ends. The practice begins.

## The game mechanics

### Each stage has:
- **A fabrication** (the boss) — what you're seeing through
- **A practice** (the weapon) — the timer-native practice for this stage
- **A text** (the map) — the Ñāṇananda section that teaches it
- **A TTS script** (the voice) — the interactive voice leading you through
- **A reward** (the loot) — what becomes visible when you see through it
- **A readiness signal** (the gate) — behavioural, not self-reported

### Progression:
- You play a stage. The TTS leads you through the practice.
- You can interrupt: "play the sound again," "skip ahead," "pause."
- Every interaction is logged.
- When you stop replaying and start resting, you're ready for the next stage.
- The runtime never says "you've attained nibbāna." It says: "What is left?
  Sit with that."

### The TTS voice:
The voice is the owner's doctrine (VBT-DOCTRINE.md). It names the
impossibility. It holds the pause. It doesn't spiritualise. It says:
"you can't force your mind to submit. you can't not try. so where does
that leave you?" And then it stops talking. And the silence is the answer.

### Repeatability:
Short plays. 30 seconds to 5 minutes. Not a sit. Play it on the bus,
in bed, between meetings. The game doesn't care if you play again or
not. The practice doesn't keep score. The stages are always there,
waiting for you to see through them.

## Why this works

Because Ñāṇananda's teaching IS a game structure:
- There are levels (the three characteristics, the signless, nibbāna)
- There are bosses (identity view, doubt, concept-making, the observer)
- There are weapons (practices: breath, sound, gap, signless)
- There is a reward (what was hidden becomes visible)
- There is a final level (nibbāna — what remains when you stop fabricating)

The only difference is that in most games, you win by defeating the boss.
In this game, you win by seeing that the boss was never real.
