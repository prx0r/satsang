## Tulkus (explainer content)
- Tulku = a recognised rebirth of a particular teacher. That's an institutional/religious recognition, not a synonym for full enlightenment.
- High tulkus are read as bodhisattvas or nirmanakaya emanations choosing rebirth out of compassion ("finished the game, came back to help"). Many tulkus are simply regarded as practitioners continuing their path, and they still need full training.
- Keep the distinctions: arhat/Buddha (liberation), bodhisattva (vow to return), tulku (recognised rebirth).
- Recognition methods: predictions, letters, dreams, divination, recognising former attendants, picking out former possessions. These are testimony-based, not controlled tests.
- The system also carried estates and lineages, so it became political (rival Karmapas, the Panchen Lama dispute).
- Example: Tsenshap Serkong Rinpoche II, born 29 May 1984 in Spiti, about 9 months after his predecessor died on 29 Aug 1983; recognised by the 14th Dalai Lama. The toddler accounts ("that's me", naming the attendant Ngawang) vary in reported age: about 2 per Berzin, nearer 4 per Rinpoche's own account.

## Tibetan cosmology (topic outline)
- Source frame: Vasubandhu's Abhidharmakosha (Kalachakra is an alternative model).
- Mount Meru, four continents (we live on Jambudvipa), billion-world systems, kalpas.
- Three realms: desire (six destinies: hells, pretas, animals, humans, asuras, gods), form (about 17 dhyana heavens, topped by the Pure Abodes) and formless (four ayatanas).
- The six desire heavens: Four Kings, Trayastrimsha, Yama, **Tushita (Ganden: Maitreya; the name behind the Gelug school and Ganden Monastery; Tsongkhapa)**, Nirmanarati, Paranirmitavashavartin (Mara).
- All of it is still samsara. Pure lands (Sukhavati, Akanishtha) are outside it.
- Inner reading: the six realms as mind-states, and Kalachakra's body-cosmos mapping. This links to the practice engine's body map.
